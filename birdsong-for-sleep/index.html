Is birdsong good for sleep? What the measurements show

Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: birdsong is a poor all-night sleep sound. In our measurements a dawn chorus had 42 loud events per minute and varied in loudness twelve times more than a mountain brook, and each call is a change the ear notices. It works well as a short wind-down sound on a timer, not as a sound to sleep through.

Birdsong sounds like the most natural sleep sound there is. Many "nature sounds for sleep" mixes include it. We recorded a dawn chorus and measured it the same way we measure every recording before it becomes a long sleep track. It failed every test, and the numbers explain why.

Loudness variation is the standard deviation of the level in 400 ms windows. A loud event is a moment more than 6 dB above the median level.

Why it keeps you awake

Birds sing in short, bright calls. In our recording there were 85 of them in two minutes. Each call is a small change in the sound, and the ear notices change. A steady sound fades into the background after a few minutes. A sound that changes every second and a half never does.

The pitch makes it worse. The centre of the sound moved between about 2,200 and 4,200 Hz, which is the range our hearing is most sensitive to. A brook sits lower and holds still.

For tinnitus masking the problem doubles. Between the calls there are quiet gaps, and in those gaps the ringing comes straight back. A good masking sound has no gaps at all.

The hidden problem: noise

Birds are small and usually far from the microphone. Our recording peaked at −38.5 dBFS, so it needed roughly 35 dB of gain to reach a normal listening level. Gain lifts everything, including the hiss of the recorder and the low rumble of distant traffic, which made up about 8% of the energy between 100 and 300 Hz. Over a full night, that background becomes part of what you hear.

When birdsong does make sense

Birdsong is a good daytime sound. It feels alive and it pulls your attention outward, which helps for a walk, a morning routine or a short break. Those same qualities are what make it a poor choice for the hours when you want your attention to switch off.

If you love birdsong at bedtime

Some people find birds the most relaxing sound there is, and that is fine. The trick is length. Use a short birdsong session while you wind down, with a timer, and let it stop before you fall asleep. Then switch to a steady sound, or silence, for the rest of the night.
