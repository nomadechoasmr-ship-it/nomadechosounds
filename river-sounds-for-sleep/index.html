River sounds for sleep: real mountain river recordings

November 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: a good river sound for sleep is one that stays the same all night. Our Mountain River, recorded in the Tian Shan near Almaty, varied in loudness by only 0.62 dB over 20 measured minutes, and no moment rose more than 10 dB above its own level. That steadiness, not the river itself, is why it works through the night.

Every river on this page is a real recording made by Andrey in the Tian Shan mountains above Almaty. Nothing is generated or added: the water is trimmed, balanced, softened slightly at the top and looped. Below are the numbers for the one river we have measured in full, the other river recordings on the channel, and practical notes on playing a river sound at night. It is listening advice, not medical advice.

What makes a river sound good for sleep

A sleep sound plays for hours while you are not listening to it. A river can be loud and still be a good night sound, as long as it does not surprise you: no sudden splash, no stone knocking, no slow swell that rises and falls. So before a river becomes a long track, we measure the take. Here is what our Mountain River showed over its middle 20 minutes (minute 4 to minute 24 of a 26.3-minute take):

MeasureMountain RiverWhat it means at night
Loudness variation0.62 dBThe level barely moves from moment to moment
Spread (P95–P5)2.04 dBQuiet and loud moments are only about 2 dB apart
Sudden peaks >6 dB0.031%A tiny share of moments stand out a little
Sudden peaks >10 dB0.000%Nothing jumps out sharply
Drift over 20 min0.6 dB RMSNo slow swell up or down
Tone centre1142–1182 HzThe character of the rush holds still
Tonal peaksnoneNo whistle or hum inside the water
Energy below 40 Hz0.04%Almost no low rumble from wind or traffic

Loudness variation is the standard deviation of the level in 400 ms windows; a sudden peak is a window more than 6 dB (or 10 dB) above the median level. Method, limits and CSV: nature sound measurement data.

The river was also recorded at a good level. Its loudest moment reached −6.2 dBFS and its average sat at −30.3 dBFS, well above the recorder's own hiss and with no clipping. The finished track needed only +2.1 dB of gain, so no background noise was lifted along with the water. The measured section is then looped; a full night track repeats it about 18 times.

River or brook?

We have measured one brook and one river the same way. Both are steady enough for a whole night, but they are not the same sound.

MeasureMountain BrookMountain River
Loudness variation0.31 dB0.62 dB
Sudden peaks >6 dBnone0.031%
Tone centre1115–1136 Hz1142–1182 Hz
Energy below 40 Hz0.059%0.04%

The numbers show something people often get wrong: the river is not a deeper sound than the brook. Its tone centre sits slightly higher, about 1,140–1,180 Hz against 1,115–1,136 Hz for the brook, and it has no more low rumble. What the river gives you is a fuller, rougher rush with more texture, while the brook is a softer, more even hiss. The brook moves half as much in loudness.

  • Choose the river if the brook feels too soft or thin, or if you want a full rush on a speaker in a room with noise outside.
  • Choose the brook if you want the most even sound we have, for example in headphones or if small changes wake you.

Speaker or headphones

On a speaker across the room, small differences in the water soften, and the room adds its own sound. That is where a river is a good start: if a softer sound gets lost against noise outside, try the river's fuller rush before you turn anything up. In headphones every detail sits right at your ears, so the most even sound is the safer choice, and the brook usually wins there. One thing we check for headphone use is the balance between left and right: the Mountain River was only 0.46 dB off straight from the recorder and got a small correction before looping.

How loud should a river sound be?

As quiet as still works for you. Start low, then raise the volume only until the river covers what bothers you, and keep it at a level that is comfortable to listen to for a long time. A river recording is already full and loud by nature, so it usually needs less volume than you expect. If you share the room, a lower level on a speaker near you is kinder than a high level across the room.

How long should it play?

That is your choice: some people like a sound all night, others prefer a timer that stops it once they are asleep. Because the measured river has no slow swells and almost no sudden peaks, it can run all night without anything in it calling for attention. The YouTube tracks are long, black-screen versions, so the screen stays dark and nothing changes on its own.

Limits

  • Only one river has been measured in full. The other river videos above are not measured yet; another river can measure differently.
  • These are acoustic measurements of the sound, not a sleep study. They show that the river is steady, not how you will sleep with it. Give one sound several nights before you judge it.
